Türkiye, daily, ex-post check

Actual vs outlook

Every net load outlook is published before the day-ahead price exists. The next day the same hours can be checked against what actually cleared, which is the only honest way to know whether the method is worth anything.

The outlook never forecasts price. What it claims is structural: which hours sit at the bottom of the range and which sit at the top. Those claims either hold or they do not, and the record below is kept whether they do or not.

What a failed check means

The method has three ways to be wrong, and each points somewhere different. If the low block lands in the right hours but the price does not follow, the bid stack is doing the work rather than the fundamentals. If the block itself lands in the wrong hours, the solar shaping is off, which is the weakest input because no forecast is published. If the evening block is right but the ramp is not, the wind forecast missed.

Recording which of the three fails is more useful than recording whether the day was right.

Track record: were we right?

Every outlook makes four claims that can be checked once prices are known: (1) the cheapest hour falls in the blue band, (2) the cheapest four hours in a row start there, (3) the dearest hour falls in the red band, (4) the dearest four hours in a row start there. The day after, each claim is marked right or wrong. Days without a red band carry only the first two claims.

For each day: the average price of its four most expensive hours in a row, minus the average of its four cheapest.
