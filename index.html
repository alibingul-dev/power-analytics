Great Britain · monthly

Wind Imbalance Scorecard

Deviation from final physical notification, imbalance cost per MWh, and directional accuracy for every transmission-connected wind BMU, built from Elexon Insights open data.

First report: September 2026 B1610 metered volumes publish five days after delivery, so the August scorecard runs in the second week of September.

What it measures

DEVIATION
Metered volume minus FPN energy, per settlement period. FPN is a MW level, so energy is (levelFrom + levelTo) ÷ 2 × 0.5 h.
COST
Deviation × system price, summed over the month. GB settles imbalance at a single price, so the direction of a miss decides whether it earns or costs.
DIRECTION
A deviation is helpful when its sign is opposite to net imbalance volume: short when the system is short, long when it is long.
SCOPE
Transmission-connected BMUs with fuel type WIND. Embedded wind sits outside the balancing mechanism. Units, not sites — Hornsea 2 reports as three BMUs.
TIMING
FPN is submitted at gate closure, one hour before delivery, so a low deviation reflects notification accuracy at gate closure rather than day-ahead forecast skill.
RUN TYPE
Interim settlement. Final reconciliation can move these figures.
Great Britain · market structure

Two markets, four differences

GB and Türkiye run the same sequence — day-ahead, intraday, balancing — but the mechanics diverge in ways that change what an imbalance actually costs.

Where the day is decided

All times local. GB closes its day-ahead auctions almost three hours earlier than Türkiye.

Four structural differences

TürkiyeGreat Britain
Settlement period1 hour, 24 per day30 minutes, 48 per day
Day-ahead venuesOne exchange, one priceTwo exchanges, two prices
Imbalance pricingDual price: min and max of MCP and SMPSingle price, so direction decides gain or loss
Price cap4,500 TL/MWh ceilingNo equivalent ceiling; negative prices occur

GB did not move to 15-minute market time units in October 2025; the change covered eleven coupled markets and excluded Great Britain and Switzerland.

Why the last row matters most

Under dual pricing, a generator that deviates is settled at whichever of the two prices is worse for it, so any deviation is a loss. Under a single price, deviating in the direction the system needs is paid. The same forecast error produces a penalty in one market and a payment in the other, which is why the same portfolio has to be managed differently on each side.
